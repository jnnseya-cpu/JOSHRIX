## Commercial Dominance Model

JOSHRIX Studio is a three-sided market: **creators** who produce games using the forge, **consumers** who purchase and play them, and **enterprises** who license the infrastructure. Each side reinforces the others: more creators → more marketplace supply → more consumers → more marketplace revenue → more enterprise interest → more licensing revenue → more platform investment → better agents → more creators.

The ACU system ensures that as the platform scales and agent intelligence improves, COGS per forge cycle decrease while operator value increases — improving unit economics at scale. These are the same dynamics that power AWS (price per compute unit falls as infrastructure scales) and frontier AI labs (model quality improves as usage scales), applied to autonomous game production.

## The Switching Doctrine — Why Anyone Leaves a $300M Game For Us

> This is the platform's official answer to "why would anyone leave Fortnite/FIFA/GTA for us?" — and the honest premise behind every product decision. **We never fight the production-value war.** A $300M game with 400 developers wins that fight forever. The winners who actually broke into gaming never fought it: Roblox (creation with friends beat AAA fidelity), YouTube (anyone can create and earn beat Hollywood), Minecraft (ownership beat graphics), Shopify (arming the ignored beat fighting the giant). People don't play "the best game" — they play for identity, belonging, creation, and money.

### The four reasons someone actually comes

1. **"It's MY game."** The switcher isn't leaving to *play* something better — they're leaving to *make* something of their own. Creation is the gameplay; the IP certificate with their name on it and the link in their group chat is a dopamine no AAA title offers. We sell authorship, not content.
2. **"It pays ME."** Elsewhere players spend and own nothing. Here the first £4.99 sale is theirs — source, IP, resale rights. **The first payout notification is the strongest retention event on the platform**; the ledger, IP registry, and payout rails matter more than any graphics engine.
3. **"It's ABOUT me."** No giant can economically make a game about a street league in Accra or a Yoruba folktale. We forge it in an hour, **in the creator's own language** — described, written, and voiced in it (see Any-Language Creation below). The long tail the giants structurally cannot serve is the moat.
4. **"It takes 10 seconds."** We don't compete for their 500 hours; we win the queue, the group chat, the fanbase drop. One shareable web link — no install, no account, no download — competes with a *message*, not with Call of Duty.

### Marketplace buyers were never their customers

The £49–£499 white-label buyer compares us to **hiring a dev studio for £30,000 and six months**, not to free games. Free games can't be owned, reskinned, or monetised — which is why every listing leads with the "Why buyers pay when free exists" ownership block.

### The four non-negotiables (product law)

1. **First-session magic** — prompt to playable in minutes, genuinely fun in 10 seconds. The QA gate is the brand; one shovelware title kills the premium story.
2. **First-payout speed** — engineer the fastest possible path to a creator's first £1. That moment converts a visitor into a resident.
3. **Zero friction to play** — the instant shareable web link is sacred. Every install step loses ~80% of the players creators bring.
4. **Social loops** — leagues, share links, template remixes. Creators bring their own audiences; the platform's job is making sharing irresistible.

### Any-Language Creation

Creation is language-first: a creator describes their game **in any language**, and the Idea Agent detects it (or honours an explicit choice) and writes every player-facing element — title, story, characters, levels, commentary — in that language, localising outward later via the culturalisation engine. This is implemented end-to-end today: the Studio's creation-language selector, the `language` field in `GameBlueprintSchema`, the gateway prompt contract, and per-language commentary voice packs (REALISM-PIPELINE §2). English-first platforms serve the global average; language-first creation serves everyone the average excludes.

### Conversion sequence

Creators wanting income → their friends and audiences as players → underserved niches and languages → business buyers. **Players follow people, not polygons.** We never ask anyone to leave their favourite game — we take the hours those games never owned, and reverse the direction money flows.
